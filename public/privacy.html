Privacy Policy

This covers Keepr Parent and Keepr Student, the two iPhone apps made by Edvocates. The short version: Keepr reads your school's Canvas data, keeps it on your phone, and doesn't have a server to lose it from. If you share with family, your messages sync through your own iCloud, not through us.

Effective September 24, 2026

Who this is about

Edvocates makes Keepr Parent (for parents and guardians) and Keepr Student (for students). Both apps connect to Canvas, the learning-management system schools use to post grades and assignments, and are built the same way under the hood. Everything in this policy applies to both apps unless we say otherwise.

Canvas is a product of Instructure, Inc. Your school, not Edvocates, runs your Canvas account and decides what data is in it. Keepr only reads what your school has already put there, using credentials you control.

What Keepr accesses

When you set up Keepr, you generate a Canvas access token, right inside the app, and hand that to Keepr instead of your Canvas password. Keepr never sees or asks for your Canvas password.

Using that token, Keepr reads, on your behalf, straight from your school's Canvas:

  • Your courses, grades, and GPA (or your kids', in Keepr Parent)
  • Assignments, due dates, and submission status
  • Class announcements
  • Basic profile info Canvas already shows you: names of linked students, teacher names, and course listings

Keepr's access is read-only. It cannot submit work, change a grade, send a message through Canvas, or alter anything in your school's system.

What Keepr doesn't do

  • No Keepr account. There's no sign-up, no email or password to create, and nothing tying your identity to us.
  • No Keepr server. Keepr talks directly to Canvas from your phone. There's no Edvocates-run backend in between that could see, log, or store your grades or your messages.
  • No analytics or tracking SDKs. We don't embed any third-party analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting tools. Any diagnostic data comes only from Apple's own optional, user-controlled device analytics, which Edvocates doesn't separately collect.
  • No selling or sharing data. We have nothing to sell, and no data-sharing arrangements with advertisers or data brokers.

Where your data lives

Your Canvas access token is stored in your iPhone's secure Keychain. Grades, assignments, and announcements that Keepr fetches are cached locally on your device so the app works quickly and offline. None of this is transmitted to, or stored by, Edvocates. If you delete Keepr from your phone, that local data goes with it.

If you turn on family sharing, the messages you share are also stored in iCloud, as described below. That's Apple's storage, tied to your Apple Account, not ours.

Notifications

Keepr can check Canvas in the background and let you know when a grade slips or something's past due. Those alerts are generated on your device from data your device already has, using iOS's own background refresh and notification tools. They don't pass through an Edvocates server to reach you.

Messages and family sharing

When you turn a grade or assignment into a message, Keepr puts it together on your device. You can send it as a text, through the Messages app or iOS's standard share sheet, or inside Keepr if your family shares. You review it and send it yourself. Keepr keeps a copy of what you sent on your phone, so you can see it later in the Messages tab.

Family sharing is optional. If a parent invites another parent or their student, Keepr uses Apple's iCloud (CloudKit) to share messages between the people who were invited. What's shared:

  • The messages and replies people send in Keepr, with the time they were sent
  • What each message was about: the class names, assignment titles, and grade details on the card
  • The name each person chooses to show (like “Mom” or “Dad”), and whether they're using Keepr Parent or Keepr Student

This is stored in the inviting parent's iCloud account and shared only with the people they invite. Edvocates can't read it. Grades themselves aren't shared this way; each parent's app reads them from Canvas with their own access.

Writing help (Apple Intelligence)

On iPhones that support Apple Intelligence, “Write it for me” can draft a message for you. The draft is written by Apple's on-device model, using what's on the card. It isn't sent to Edvocates, and you can edit or delete the draft before anything is sent.

Subscriptions

Keepr Parent is a subscription with a free trial, and Keepr Student is free. Purchases, trials, and renewals are handled by Apple through the App Store. Edvocates never receives your payment details. Keepr checks whether you're subscribed on your device, using Apple's StoreKit.

Sample data mode

Both apps let you look around with made-up sample students and grades, with no Canvas account required. That data is fabricated on your device and never touches a real Canvas system or Edvocates.

Children's privacy

Keepr Student is intended for middle and high school students, generally 13 and older, and Keepr Parent is intended for adult parents and guardians. Neither app is directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. In practice, since Keepr doesn't collect or transmit personal information to Edvocates at all, regardless of the user's age, there's no personal information about any student, of any age, sitting on our servers to protect, because we don't have servers holding it.

If you're a parent or school administrator with a concern about a student's use of Keepr, contact us at the address below.

Security

Keepr talks to Canvas over encrypted HTTPS connections and stores your access token in iOS's Keychain rather than in plain app storage. Because your data stays on your device (and, if you share with family, in iCloud), the main way to keep it secure is the same way you keep the rest of your phone secure: a passcode or Face ID/Touch ID, and keeping iOS up to date.

Your choices

  • Revoke access anytime. Delete the access token from your Canvas account settings, and Keepr immediately loses the ability to read anything new.
  • Delete your data. Deleting the app removes everything Keepr stored on your phone. There's no account or server copy to separately ask us to delete. Messages shared with family stay in iCloud until the parent who set up sharing stops sharing.
  • Stop sharing. The parent who set up family sharing can remove someone or stop sharing entirely from Settings, and anyone who was invited can leave. Either way, it's done in the app.
  • Turn off notifications. Use iOS Settings to control what Keepr can alert you about, same as any other app.

Changes to this policy

If how Keepr handles data changes, we'll update this page and change the effective date above. We'll avoid legalese where we can, for the same reason Keepr avoids nagging you: nobody reads the fine print if it isn't short.
